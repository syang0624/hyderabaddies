# Hironebito-san interview guide (evaluator side)

Sat Sep 26. Name as Gemini transcribed it from Ryo (12-20 02:40): mentor, former engineering lead at Meta. Check the spelling on his badge. He may be the "Hiro" with the lunch-mentoring reservation (Golden Gates 1F, 1:30 slot); if so, this is that slot.

Why him: the VP gave the top-down view, Ryo the evaluated side. Hironebito is the third seat: a manager who has staffed teams and judged engineers, and an engineer who will poke the technical 30%.

Open with: "Can I record this for our notes? Audio only, for transcription." Then the 7 steps. Laptop stays closed. The running UI (localhost:8787) is a last resort: only if he asks to see it, or if step 6 stalls with nothing concrete to react to. Interview evidence first, demo never before step 6.

### 1. Who
"At Meta, how many people did you evaluate or staff a year, and who evaluated you?"

### 2. The last time
"Walk me through the last time you had to pick one person for a team or a role. What did you actually look at?"
Follow-up: "How much of that was things you could point to, and how much was what you'd heard about them?"

### 3. Where it went wrong
"Tell me about a time you staffed someone and got it wrong. What did you see afterwards that you wish you'd seen before?"
"When a peer or a skip-level described someone to you, how often did that turn out to be off?"

### 4. Workaround today
"How did you protect yourself from that? Reference checks, reading their diffs, a trial project, a doc?"
(Meta has PSC with peer feedback and self-review. Ask: "Did people's self-reviews or peer packets actually change your call, or did you decide first and read second?")

### 5. Magic wand
"If you could have had one thing on the table before that decision, what would it have been?"

### 6. Reveal, as a question
"We're building a page for one decision: who goes on a team or a posting. It pulls what each person wrote and shipped, from sources a policy allows, every line links to its source, no score, and the person sees the same page and can contest a line. Would that have changed the call you got wrong, or is it just more reading?"
Then the technical probe: "What would make you not believe this?" and "Where does this break at 3,000 managers?" If, and only if, he can't picture it from words, open the laptop on Rin and hand him the trackpad.

### 7. Close
"Who at Meta owned this problem, and what did they buy or build?" "Who else here should we ask?"

### Listen for
- A concrete wrong-hire or wrong-staffing story (step 3). That is the pitch anecdote if he lets us use it.
- Whether he decided first and read the packet second (step 4). If yes, the product has to reach him before he decides, which argues for the Slack intake.
- His break-it answer (step 6). Put it in the Q&A prep verbatim.

### Do not
- Mention the VP's figures, the pilot, or Recruit's internal prototype.
- Pitch before step 6.
