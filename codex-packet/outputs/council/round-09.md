### Ten-year ambition, one-day mechanism

The imaginative future is that access to experience becomes less dependent on knowing a patient expert, being in the right city, or having spare mentor time. For onboarding, the route begins with one supervisor-approved situation that a newcomer can rehearse privately. Career exploration is a separate possible future, not this weekend's sales story. This preserves the Odyssey's ambition while giving one observable mechanism priority.

The one-day prototype would contain a fictional hotel front desk, one reservation question, one authoritative scenario package, three permitted actions, a mutable room-readiness fact, grounded dialogue, and a resettable action trace. The learner can inspect the record, make an unsupported promise, or ask for supervisor help. An unsupported promise changes the simulated state to an unresolved promise; it does not fabricate a satisfied guest. A changed readiness fact should make a different response appropriate. There are no real reservations, payments, safety decisions, or employee evaluations.

Fictional case details are **seeded, not expert-approved** until an actual consenting domain practitioner reviews them. A language toggle is not proof of multilingual fidelity. The demo can show one reviewed language pair if a qualified reviewer is available; otherwise present language support as a roadmap item and demonstrate the consequence engine in the team's working language. Do not imply English and Japanese versions are equivalent merely because a model translated them.

Build the case and state transitions first, then a functional chat/inbox, then source-grounded explanations, then one changed-condition run. Add a tiny room interface only if it makes the work legible; the core must remain usable as text with keyboard controls. Cut movement, avatars, voice, broad authoring, integrations, and multi-industry coverage before cutting correctness. The worker-value hypothesis does not depend on animated coworkers.

## Critique-driven revision and reversal conditions

**06.1 → 09.0:** Preserve B's strongest mechanism and incumbent comparison. Introduce the MHLW context with its correct denominator. Explore C's access-to-practice benefit outside recruiting, without treating the survey as evidence for our intervention.

**09.0 → 09.1 after fictional deliberation:** Split C's buyers; select onboarding only. Give expert approval power to reverse the build choice. Remove automatic multilingual equivalence. Count content maintenance and learner time. Make privacy a concrete implementation requirement rather than a slogan. Preserve B's score and Real-time Systems dissent. Explicitly disclose the reused-agent method limitation.

Three gates decide the next move:

1. **Approve and observe one case.** A supervisor and learner describe a recent recurring situation and what remains hard after current training. A qualified reviewer approves the narrow content. Without that, C remains a fictional mechanism demo and B leads the build decision if its core can execute.
2. **Beat the simpler alternative on the right outcome.** Compare C against the same case and feedback without the world, then try a new case. Include learner understanding, errors, expert time, practice time, and trust. Translation alone or supervisor role-play may win. No employment or causal outcome claim follows from one participant.
3. **Scale through evidence, not analogy.** Validate another case before another industry. Retain separate buyer hypotheses. B overtakes if the recurring comparison bottleneck and competent baseline advantage become clearer; A overtakes if a meaningful recipient-safe handoff burden survives the existing tools. If none does, recommend none as a business yet.

**Round recommendation:** Rehearse C-onboarding for its direct learner-value hypothesis, with B as a close and technically stronger fallback. The ten-year ambition is broad access to experience. The one-day obligation is one inspectable action, consequence, and explanation whose professional meaning someone qualified has actually reviewed.
