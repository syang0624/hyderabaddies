## Final script — 6 minutes, approximately 657 spoken words

[0:00–0:25 — Title and problem.]

**Presenter:** “Imagine your first difficult moment in a new role. Someone wants a quick answer. The important clue is available, but you do not yet know to look for it. We propose Work Rehearsal: a small workplace where you can practice noticing, asking and acting before the consequences are real.”

[0:25–0:55 — Inspiration, with explicit evidence boundary.]

“Our interviews showed real workflows and capable people already using AI. Shion prepares candidate handoffs; Koki already makes prototypes to resolve product questions. Neither interview validates this proposal. They pushed us toward a different question: where would trying the work teach something that reading a summary cannot? That is our hypothesis, and today we can propose a bounded way to test it.”

[0:55–2:15 — Solution demonstration. Concept sequence until a genuine artifact exists; pause for learner action.]

“The scene would be one fictional service desk. A coworker asks you to restart a service. There is a status card, and it says the batch is active. You can inspect, restart, or wait. The rule belongs only to this invented exercise: restarting an active batch interrupts it. Waiting completes the batch; restarting after completion restores service.

“If you restart immediately, the scene should change because the engine records an interruption. A fixed result card would show the action, prior state and applicable rule. You could ask why. The language model would explain those supplied facts, while the source remains available to inspect.

“Now you change the starting status to complete and repeat the restart. The outcome should change to restoration. This is the moment we want you to control. The system would calculate the result from your input; it would not play a success video selected by the presenter.

“You could reset and try again. We would not turn your mistake into an employability percentage. The purpose is exploration and practice, with an attempt artifact you can inspect.”

[2:15–3:10 — Impact and buyer hypothesis.]

“A possible buyer is an onboarding manager who repeatedly explains the same judgment to newcomers. That manager is a hypothesis, not a customer. We have no expert-approved case, measured learning benefit or revenue. Our current fictional case could test software behavior, but it could not establish professional training quality.

“The next useful experiment would compare an approved case in this interface with the same information in a plain document or branching exercise. Then participants would attempt a fresh case. We would measure understanding and transfer, and count expert preparation, corrections and maintenance. For illustration only, four hours of content work reused twenty times means twelve minutes of authoring per attempt, before support or model costs. Reuse is not guaranteed, and completion is not proof of learning.”

[3:10–3:50 — Differentiation.]

“Existing job simulations already make work accessible. Expertise chatbots already answer questions. Our proposed difference is the combination of attempted work, inspectable consequences and an explanation tied to the exact attempt. That difference must survive comparison with simpler tools. A cute room alone is not innovation, and an AI coworker speaking fluently is not evidence that we captured an expert.”

[3:50–5:10 — Technical design and feasibility.]

“The build boundary is two people and one day. We would start with a transition table in a plain interface. One person owns state, actions and the event log; the other owns the scenario wording, source cards and presentation. Only after the two starting conditions produce correct different outcomes would we add the scene and explanation.

“Code owns state. The model receives the current event and applicable rule and returns explanatory language. It cannot change consequences. If inference is unavailable, the direct rule remains usable. That fallback would demonstrate the engine, not successful AI behavior. The art is one desk whose appearance reflects the state; no navigation, multiplayer, voice cloning or generated worlds.”

[5:10–6:00 — Roadmap and closing action.]

“Before a professional pilot, a willing expert must approve one real recurring case. We would keep revision and withdrawal possible, and investigate whether maintaining accurate scenarios costs more than repeated coaching. If the content economics fail or a document teaches just as well, we would narrow or stop. The weekend's honest proof would be smaller: one situation, your decision, and a consequence that changes when the evidence changes.”

## Two-person day: minimum, costs and abandonment

Planning budget only: ten effective elapsed hours means at most twenty person-hours, before interruptions. Do not convert calendar availability into guaranteed productive time. The event forbids pre-opening personal code; any reuse must respect that rule and OSS disclosure requirements, as documented in the frozen judges research. Inspect what was created during the permitted window before rebuilding.

| Elapsed planning window | Builder 1 | Builder 2 | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 hour | Agree state/action schema | Write fictional rule cards and storyboard | Both independently predict every supported outcome; disagreement means scenario is not ready. |
| 1–3 | Plain engine, reset, log, invalid input | Plain scene layout and exact wording against schema | Two starting statuses and wait/restart paths work without a model. |
| 3–5 | Wire bounded explanation and unavailable state | Connect scene to state; direct rule/result cards | Scene and log agree; model cannot mutate state. |
| 5–6 | Inspect transitions, repeated reset and bad input | Inspect explanations, wording and readability | Record what actually passes; cut model if unreliable. |
| 6–7 | Correct failures | One visual polish pass | One expressive change per state; no new mechanics. |
| 7–9 | Public-safe repository and technical evidence | Deck, timed speech and real recording if possible | Every claim maps to an observed artifact or explicit hypothesis. |
| 9–10 | Shared buffer and final consistency review | Shared buffer and final consistency review | No expansion. Concept-only if key mechanism still fails. |

**Scenario authoring is a critical path.** Even fiction requires a consistent rule, discoverable clue, plausible pressure, reversible learning opportunity, explanations for alternatives and a defined ending. Budget the first hour for agreement and the second builder's next two hours for writing/layout, with correction time later. These are caps, not evidence a qualified scenario can be made in three hours. Real professional review is absent and could dwarf software effort.

**Preserve delight:** one coworker with a short urgent request; a status change the learner causes; an interruption that visibly halts the desk; a successful restart that restores activity; a quick reset that invites a different choice. Use typography, one color transition and a brief authored reaction. Do not add XP, leaderboard or timed punishment to manufacture engagement.

**Abandoned scope:** office navigation, pathfinding, 3D, multiplayer, avatar creator, multiple coworkers, agent-to-agent chat, voice input/output, face or voice likeness, arbitrary text actions, generic scenario generation, integrations, personal data, competency scoring, employer matching, billing and second scenario. Export and persistent accounts are also unnecessary. “Learner controls sharing” remains a future requirement; the minimum simply has no external sharing function.

If the plain engine is not functioning by hour three, spend the next block fixing or simplifying it rather than decorating. If model explanations are unreliable by hour six, cut them and lower the claim. If no working engine exists by recording time, use labeled concept frames and accept the lack of PoC evidence. Do not pretend the minimum is achieved because the file tree exists.
