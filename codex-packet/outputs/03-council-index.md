# Ten council rehearsals

**Fictional critiques for preparation. These are not actual judges' statements, interviews, preferences or predicted scores.** Eight distinct council agents handle ten separate assignments. The agent tool reached its thread limit; rounds09 and10 reuse completed council agents in new turns, explicitly disclosed in their lineage. Within each assignment, one agent authored the presenter and all four critic voices; this was not a live debate among four separate judge agents. All use related models, a shared evidence packet and common instructions; repeated agreement is not independent market evidence. The useful output is the objection, revision and test—not a vote count.

Each linked transcript includes a drafted preliminary pitch, preliminary Q&A, final pitch, final Q&A, 90-second demo storyboard, four-lens deliberation, comparative score table, demo truth table and reversal conditions. The scripts are drafted for Carl and Steven, not transcripts of anything they said. All demo behavior remains proposed until implemented and inspected.

The lenses are Workflow & Scale, Worker Value & Business, Real-time Systems, and Enterprise AI Reliability. Their relevance comes from the public backgrounds of the announced judges; dialogue is generated rather than attributed to those people. The actual event weights are impact40, creativity30 and technical30. Numerical scores here are subjective concept comparisons, not empirical results or winning probabilities.

## Method and revision lineage

Rounds01–03 independently compare the initial shared packet. Rounds04–06 revise those baselines under specific stress. Rounds07–09 stress the revised paths. Round10 compares the resulting arguments. This is a branched rehearsal process, not ten successive customer-validation cycles. The newer Japanese communication survey is an explicitly marked optional challenger in later rounds; it does not retroactively validate the baseline interviews.

All three candidates remain in every comparison: A recipient-appropriate Handoff; B conversation-to-Live Experiment; C interactive Work Rehearsal. Related variations change the buyer and product, so their scores are not perfectly comparable. A recommendation to investigate A first can coexist with a higher creative-potential score for C.

## What the rehearsals actually resolved

The most useful outcome is three different answers: **A is closest to an observed workflow; B has the cleanest live transformation; C carries the strongest longer-term learning/access ambition.** The rounds made the demo mechanisms and failure conditions more concrete. They did not discover a paying customer or establish learning gains.

For C, pick one buyer and one task. A company onboarding product needs a recurring training problem, a qualified case reviewer and an operations/training budget owner. A career-preview product needs learner interest, accurate role representation and a sponsor who values informed choices. Neither gains demand evidence from the other's story.

For B, the real demonstration is a judge-authored constraint changing functioning alternatives consistently. For C, it is a learner's action changing a consequence, then a changed fact changing the result and explanation. For A, it is a source or recipient change producing the correct, reviewable briefing change. Seeded inputs are compatible with all three; predetermined outputs cannot prove the claimed live transformation.

The stopping rule is practical: get the one missing operator fact, inspect a running core mechanism, and compare against the best existing workaround. Do not run more friendly councils to compensate for missing customer evidence.

## Read strategically

Start with round10 for the final synthesis, round06 for the strongest live-experiment challenger, and round03 for the incumbent argument. The remaining rounds preserve the full reasoning and revisions. Their scores use the same headline weights but shift concepts and assumptions; do not average them or treat a one-point gap as meaningful.
