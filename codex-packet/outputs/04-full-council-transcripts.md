“Here is the prototype we would test. A fictional colleague reports that a deployment has increased errors. You inspect the evidence and choose what to do. Restart immediately, and the simulation shows the consequence under its explicit rules. Investigate first, and a clue changes your options.

“Ask, ‘Why not restart?’ The guide would answer from an approved explanation and expose its source. Then change one condition: the retry queue is empty. The same action should now produce a different outcome where the rules justify it. An unsupported question should produce an honest boundary, not invented expertise.”

**1:50–2:15 — Impact.**

“An illustrative pilot calculation is twenty repeated ten-minute explanations: two hundred expert minutes. That is a hypothetical baseline, not savings. We must subtract authoring and review time, then test whether learners perform better on a different scenario.”

**2:15–2:30 — Differentiation.**

“Forage offers work simulations; Delphi offers interactive expertise. Our proposed distinction is joining source-grounded explanation to consequences from the learner’s actual actions.”

**2:30–2:50 — Technical design. [Show state → rules → retrieval → dialogue.]**

“Software owns state and consequences. The model explains and interprets questions within that state. Experts approve the scenario and rationale; the model does not become the expert.”

**2:50–3:00 — Roadmap.**

“First prove one changed-condition rehearsal. Then test learning, author effort and a buyer’s willingness to sponsor it. Learners choose what they share.”

## Final — six-minute spoken draft

**DRAFTED FOR CARL/STEVEN. FICTIONAL SIMULATION. 709 spoken words; pauses and storyboard actions fill 360 seconds.**

**0:00–0:10 — Title. [Learner, colleague, one problem.]**

“Work Rehearsal is a proposal to make professional experience something more people can practice. Try the decision, see the consequence, then understand the expert’s reasoning.”

**0:10–0:50 — Problem.**

“A student can spend hours researching an engineering career without experiencing its smallest everyday judgments. A service is failing. A colleague wants a quick fix. The evidence is incomplete. Do you restart, investigate, or escalate?

“The people with useful experience cannot personally rehearse every situation with every newcomer. But a recording of their advice may not tell a learner whether they understood it. We want to investigate that gap, starting with one incident for a first-time software intern.”

**0:50–1:20 — Inspiration.**

“Our interviews gave us two actual workflows: preparing recruiting handoffs and turning product disagreements into prototypes. They also challenged our assumptions. Existing AI tools already help, and frustration was sometimes lower than our questions implied.

“So this is a new hypothesis, not a discovery we attribute to those interviewees: an expert might teach more effectively by authoring a small decision environment. A learner could try first and ask for help when the reasoning becomes meaningful.”

**1:20–3:00 — Solution. [Run only if built; otherwise explicitly show labeled concept storyboard.]**

“The proposed demonstration starts in a tiny fictional office. You are the intern. A teammate reports rising errors after a deployment and asks for an update. You have logs, a status panel, and colleagues you can question.

“You choose an action. If you restart with pending work in the queue, the scenario’s rules produce a specified consequence. This is an educational model, not a claim about every real system. You can inspect the rule and replay the case.

“Now ask the guide, ‘What should I have noticed?’ It would identify the relevant clue, retrieve the approved rationale, and show the link between that evidence and your choice. It would not say, ‘This is exactly what a real employee would think.’

“Next, someone changes a supported condition live: the queue is empty. The engine should update the outcome. The explanation must also update; it cannot keep repeating the earlier warning. Ask about something outside the approved scenario and it should say what it cannot answer.

“The learner leaves with an action trace and a question for a real mentor. They may reset and practice privately. They choose whether to share their artifact. We propose no personality score or hiring recommendation.”

**3:00–3:55 — Impact. [Show one transparent calculation and the evaluation plan.]**

“Consider an illustrative baseline: twenty learners each need ten minutes of the same explanation. That is two hundred expert minutes. This is arithmetic with invented planning inputs, not an observed result or promised saving.

“The actual test includes the cost of authoring, reviewing and correcting the rehearsal. More importantly, we would compare transfer: can a learner explain and handle a different scenario better than after reading the same source material? We would measure both errors and appropriate requests for help.

“Our first buyer hypothesis is an early-career program sponsoring a free learner experience. That is not validated pricing. A sponsor must value clearer role understanding, and learners must find the rehearsal worth their time.”

**3:55–4:30 — Differentiation.**

“Forage already offers employer-designed simulations. Delphi already makes expertise conversational. A virtual office alone does not differentiate us. Our proposed contribution is the connection between a learner’s real action, an inspectable consequence, and a grounded explanation that changes when the facts change. We would compare directly with a document and a simple branching exercise.”

**4:30–5:30 — Technical design. [Keep architecture visible.]**

“The architecture has four parts: an approved scenario package; deterministic state and transition rules; retrieval over approved explanations; and a dialogue layer that receives only the permitted state and sources.

“The model can interpret a question and draft an explanation. It cannot silently rewrite the incident’s truth. Actions must map to supported commands, and every state change is logged. An ambiguous command asks for clarification. A delayed model response leaves the evidence and controls usable. An unavailable model produces a visible failure, not a fake answer.

“We would test paired cases where one fact changes, invalid actions, contradictory evidence, and unsupported questions. A successful scripted path alone would not establish reliability.”

**5:30–6:00 — Roadmap.**

“First, establish that one real expert wants to author one recurring situation. Second, implement and inspect the changed-condition mechanism. Third, compare learning and total expert effort against existing materials. Expand only if those tests support it. The ambition is access to useful practice without requiring everyone to already know someone in the profession.”

Source notes: [Forage simulations](https://www.theforage.com/simulations), [Delphi](https://www.delphi.ai/). No external productivity percentage is used as product impact. Macro labor forecasts are deliberately excluded from market sizing. The illustrative calculation must remain labeled on the visual.

### Presenter recovery ladder, prepared before taking the stage
