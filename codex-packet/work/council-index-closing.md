
## What the rehearsals actually resolved

The most useful outcome is three different answers: **A is closest to an observed workflow; B has the cleanest live transformation; C carries the strongest longer-term learning/access ambition.** The rounds made the demo mechanisms and failure conditions more concrete. They did not discover a paying customer or establish learning gains.

For C, pick one buyer and one task. A company onboarding product needs a recurring training problem, a qualified case reviewer and an operations/training budget owner. A career-preview product needs learner interest, accurate role representation and a sponsor who values informed choices. Neither gains demand evidence from the other's story.

For B, the real demonstration is a judge-authored constraint changing functioning alternatives consistently. For C, it is a learner's action changing a consequence, then a changed fact changing the result and explanation. For A, it is a source or recipient change producing the correct, reviewable briefing change. Seeded inputs are compatible with all three; predetermined outputs cannot prove the claimed live transformation.

The stopping rule is practical: get the one missing operator fact, inspect a running core mechanism, and compare against the best existing workaround. Do not run more friendly councils to compensate for missing customer evidence.
